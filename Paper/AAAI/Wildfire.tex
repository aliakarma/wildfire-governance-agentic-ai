\begin{abstract}
Constrained Markov decision process (CMDP) methods enforce safety through Lagrangian relaxation and therefore permit constraint violations, an unacceptable failure mode for public warning systems. We introduce the \emph{Governance-Invariant MDP} (GOMDP), a system design that enforces safety constraints at the environment level as cryptographic state-transition invariants rather than as policy penalties, instantiated for wildfire early warning with a Hyperledger Fabric smart contract encoding mandatory human authorization. We treat the formal component as a system-level security argument rather than a new learning-theoretic result: under stated assumptions (Ed25519 signature security, Byzantine validator threshold $k \geq 3f{+}1$, faithful chaincode), any policy, however suboptimal or adversarial, satisfies the governance predicate with probability negligibly close to one (Theorem~1), and a closed-form breach bound under validator compromise identifies where Byzantine consensus does and does not outperform a single signature-verifying server (Theorem~2). The paper's central empirical contribution is a layered decomposition isolating what each enforcement layer buys: a PPO policy trained inside the GOMDP is evaluated against constrained RL (PPO-CMDP, WCSAC), cryptography-free environment enforcement (logical shielding, SafeLayer), and a centralized signature-verifying server that separates authentication from consensus. Across 20 seeds, PPO-GOMDP reduces detection latency by 17.5\% relative to a training-free greedy coordination baseline (Greedy-GOMDP, $18.3\to15.1$ steps) at 100\% governance compliance, and cuts false public alerts from 22.4\% under an ungoverned adaptive baseline to 6.0\%, with no significant latency difference from PPO-CMDP (paired $t(19)=1.28$, $p=0.22$; TOST-confirmed equivalence within a 1-step margin, max one-sided $p=0.004$). Evaluations on three VIIRS-derived wildfire events and adversarial stress tests, including validator compromise and a strategic belief-targeted spoofing attack, complete the safety case.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Safety-critical agentic AI systems need guarantees that hold for every trajectory, under adversarial conditions, independently of policy optimality. Constrained MDPs~\cite{altman1999} and trust-region methods such as CPO~\cite{achiam2017} enforce constraints in expectation via Lagrangian relaxation, so violation remains common (5--15\% empirically even after convergence~\cite{achiam2017}); more recent projection-based updates~\cite{yang2020pcpo}, distributional critics~\cite{yang2021wcsac}, and safety layers~\cite{dalal2018} reduce but cannot eliminate violations, since all such methods tie their guarantee to policy or model quality~\cite{gu2022survey}. For disaster warning, where one false alert can trigger mass evacuation and one missed detection can cost lives, a probabilistic guarantee is insufficient.

We address this gap with the \emph{Governance-Invariant MDP} (GOMDP), in which the safety constraint is embedded in the environment's transition function via cryptographic enforcement rather than in the policy's reward signal. The structural insight is a separation of concerns: \emph{safety is an environment property, not a policy property}. Any policy, random, greedy, or fully trained, satisfies the governance constraint with probability negligibly close to one, because the constraint is a cryptographic state-transition invariant no action can bypass. We are explicit about the genre of this contribution: a \emph{formally scoped safety case} for a governed agentic system, whose formal results identify the precise assumptions under which the central guarantee holds, rather than a new learning-theoretic object; the experiments, which test the system against the strongest available policy-level and environment-level enforcement baselines, carry the paper's primary evidentiary weight.

We instantiate the framework for wildfire early warning~\cite{divirgilio2019,ghali2022}: a Hyperledger Fabric~\cite{androulaki2018} smart contract refuses to execute the alert-broadcast transition unless a cryptographically valid human authorization signature is present, turning human oversight from a procedural check into an environment constraint. The architecture integrates multi-agent UAV coordination (a risk-weighted greedy policy and a deep PPO policy trained inside the GOMDP) with two-stage Bayesian cross-modal anomaly verification.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} \textbf{(C1)}~The GOMDP framework: governance-invariant MDPs with cryptographic environment-level enforcement, positioned relative to shielding, safety layers, and control barrier functions. \textbf{(C2)}~A layered empirical decomposition separating the marginal contribution of cryptographic authentication from that of Byzantine consensus (the Central+Sig ablation), absent from prior shielding and safety-layer evaluations: PPO-GOMDP achieves 17.5\% lower detection latency than the training-free Greedy-GOMDP baseline at 100\% compliance, with TOST-confirmed latency equivalence to PPO-CMDP and categorically stronger compliance (100\% vs.\ 92.8\%/90.6\%). \textbf{(C3)}~A system-level security argument rather than a new learning-theoretic result (Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}), identifying the minimal assumption set under which any policy satisfies the governance predicate with probability negligibly close to one. \textbf{(C4)}~A closed-form breach bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:adversarial}) with an honest operating envelope for when consensus does and does not outperform a single signature-verifying server. \textbf{(C5)}~A broad evaluation against logical shielding and SafeLayer, covering three VIIRS-derived events with baselines on each, and strategic as well as i.i.d.\ adversaries.


\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Constrained RL and Safe AI} Altman's CMDP framework~\cite{altman1999} enforces constraints via Lagrangian relaxation, providing in-expectation guarantees; CPO~\cite{achiam2017} and RCPO~\cite{tessler2019} reduce but cannot eliminate violations, and PCPO~\cite{yang2020pcpo} projects updates onto the constraint set without removing them. WCSAC~\cite{yang2021wcsac} adds a distributional (CVaR-based) safety critic (90.6\% compliance in our evaluation) but remains policy-level. SafeLayer~\cite{dalal2018} projects actions per step via a learned linearized cost model, stronger than Lagrangian methods but inheriting model error (Section~\ref{sec:simresults}). Safe shielding~\cite{alshiekh2018} filters unsafe actions at the environment boundary, architecturally closest to GOMDP; our shield baseline shows this enforcement is complete against the policy's own actions yet defenseless against external injection. Surveys~\cite{gu2022survey} confirm per-trajectory guarantees under adversarial conditions remain open for policy-level methods; GOMDP extends shielding with cryptographic enforcement and Byzantine-tolerant consensus, a threat model absent from prior shielding work.

\paragraph{Runtime Assurance, CBFs, and Monitoring} The Simplex architecture~\cite{sha2001} pioneered environment-boundary enforcement in aerospace: a verified safety controller overrides an unverified performance controller when a safety envelope is threatened. GOMDP inherits this philosophy but replaces the trusted local monitor with a distributed cryptographic mechanism, changing the threat model from component failure to adversarial compromise. Control Barrier Functions~\cite{ames2019} enforce forward-invariant safe sets for continuous dynamics; the GOMDP predicate is discrete and requires non-repudiation beyond CBF synthesis. Runtime monitoring~\cite{leucker2009} observes and reacts to events, whereas GOMDP embeds enforcement in the transition function itself; shield synthesis from LTL specifications~\cite{alshiekh2018} is orthogonal to how the resulting predicate is enforced at runtime.

\paragraph{AI-Based Wildfire Detection} Contemporary systems apply deep learning and multimodal fusion~\cite{ghali2022}. IoT threshold pipelines~\cite{tarigan2026} report mean detection latency near 45 time units without adaptive patrol, consistent with our Static baseline ($41.5$~steps; context only). Partheepan~et~al.~\cite{partheepan2023} identify governance and accountability as open challenges in autonomous UAV monitoring, the gap GOMDP addresses; constrained safe RL for autonomous driving~\cite{gao2024} tackles a related domain with soft Lagrangian constraints.

\paragraph{Blockchain Accountability and Verified Contracts} Permissioned blockchains such as Hyperledger Fabric enable authenticated, tamper-evident execution with Byzantine fault-tolerant ordering, while prior blockchain-based disaster monitoring primarily uses distributed ledgers for post-hoc auditing rather than real-time governance enforcement. Assumption~\ref{ass:impl} connects our guarantee to the smart-contract verification literature~\cite{bhargavan2016}. Human-in-the-loop architectures reduce automation error in safety-critical domains~\cite{holzinger2025}; in contrast, GOMDP models oversight as a cryptographic environment constraint rather than an interface-level validation step.


\section{The Governance-Invariant MDP Framework}
\label{sec:gomdp}

We introduce the GOMDP as a system design for environment-level safety enforcement, together with a formal security argument that makes explicit the assumptions under which the design's central guarantee holds.

\subsection{Formal Definition}
\label{sec:def}

\begin{definition}[Governance-Invariant MDP]
	\label{def:gomdp}
	A Governance-Invariant MDP ($\GOMDP$) is a tuple $(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{T}_G, r, \Omega, O, \mathcal{G})$, where $\mathcal{S}$, $\mathcal{A}$, $r$, $\Omega$, $O$ are standard POMDP components with per-step reward $r: \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A} \to \Rset$, $\mathcal{G}: \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A} \to \{0,1\}$ is a \emph{governance predicate}, and $\mathcal{T}_G$ is the governance-constrained transition function: $\mathcal{T}_G(s' \mid s, a) = \mathcal{T}(s' \mid s, a)$ if $\mathcal{G}(s, a) = 1$ or $a \notin \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{alert}}$, and $\mathcal{T}_G(\text{block} \mid s, a) = 1$ (the alert action is blocked) if $a \in \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{alert}}$ and $\mathcal{G}(s, a) = 0$. This makes $\mathcal{T}_G$ total over $\mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A}$: the governance predicate gates only alert actions, and every non-alert action proceeds under the unconstrained kernel $\mathcal{T}$ regardless of $\mathcal{G}$. The enforcement of $\mathcal{G}$ is \emph{cryptographic}: the environment refuses to execute alert state transitions lacking a cryptographically valid governance certificate, independently of the policy.
\end{definition}

The distinction from a CMDP is structural: a CMDP constraint modifies the learning signal via a Lagrange multiplier, so a sufficiently suboptimal or adversarial policy can still act unsafely, whereas a $\GOMDP$ environment cannot produce a state with $\text{Alert} = 1$ unless $\mathcal{G}(s,a) = 1$. This also separates GOMDP from logical shielding~\cite{alshiekh2018}, which lacks cryptographic non-repudiation and BFT consensus, and from SafeLayer~\cite{dalal2018}, whose learned projection carries model error. The structural separation, not a new cryptographic primitive, is the central claim; the formal result below pins down the assumptions it rests on.

\begin{assumption}[Faithful Implementation]
	\label{ass:impl}
	The deployed smart-contract chaincode is a correct, bug-free realization of $\mathcal{T}_G$ as specified in Definition~\ref{def:gomdp}: for every $(s, a)$ with $a \in \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{alert}}$, the chaincode evaluates $\mathcal{G}(s,a)$ and blocks the transition whenever $\mathcal{G}(s,a) = 0$. Formal program verification of the chaincode, for which established techniques exist~\cite{bhargavan2016}, is outside the scope of this paper; we treat this assumption explicitly and return to its consequences in the Limitations.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Policy-Agnostic Safety}
\label{sec:theorem1}

\begin{theorem}[Policy-Agnostic Safety]
	\label{thm:safety}
	Let $\Sigma = (\mathcal{G}\text{-MDP}, \Pi_{\mathrm{Ed25519}}, \mathcal{V})$ be a GOMDP instantiation satisfying Assumption~\ref{ass:impl}, where $\Pi_{\mathrm{Ed25519}}$ is a $(t_{\mathrm{adv}}, \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}})$-secure digital signature scheme~\cite{bernstein2012ed25519} and $\mathcal{V}$ is a set of $k$ validators of which at most $f$ are Byzantine, with $k \geq 3f + 1$ (equivalently, $f \leq \lfloor (k-1)/3 \rfloor$). Let $\pi$ be any stochastic policy (including arbitrarily suboptimal or adversarially chosen policies) operating within $\Sigma$ with governance predicate $\mathcal{G}(s, a) = [\Conf_t^{(2)} > \tau \wedge \HA_t = 1]$. Then, for any adversary running in time $t \leq t_{\mathrm{adv}}$ and for all horizons $T$:
	\begin{equation}
		P_\pi\!\left(\text{Alert}_t = 1 \;\Big|\; \Conf_t^{(2)} \leq \tau \;\vee\; \HA_t = 0\right) \leq T \cdot \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}},
		\label{eq:safety}
	\end{equation}
	where $T \cdot \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}} \approx 0$ under standard hardness assumptions for Ed25519 ($\epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}} \approx 2^{-128}$). Conditional on Assumption~\ref{ass:impl}, the guarantee is independent of $\pi$ and holds with probability negligibly close to one over all trajectories.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
	By reduction: an adversary producing an unauthorized alert must, under Assumption~\ref{ass:impl}, either forge a valid certificate without an authorized validator key (Case 1) or corrupt more than $f$ validators (Case 2, handled by Theorem~\ref{thm:adversarial}). Case 1 yields an Ed25519 forger with the same success probability, bounded by $\epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}}$; a union bound over $T$ timesteps gives~\eqref{eq:safety}. The proof does not rule out a chaincode bug; that failure mode is excluded by hypothesis (Assumption~\ref{ass:impl}), so \eqref{eq:safety} is a guarantee about the specification conditional on faithful implementation, not an unconditional guarantee about any deployed artifact. The full proof appears in the technical appendix.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Safety--Optimality Decoupling]
	\label{cor:decoupling}
	The guarantee of Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} holds for any policy $\pi$, including the worst-case policy that minimizes return; the optimality gap does not affect the safety certificate. This contrasts with CMDP/CPO/WCSAC, where constraint violation probability depends directly on the optimality gap during training~\cite{achiam2017,yang2021wcsac}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Novelty and Scope]
	\label{rem:scope}
	We state plainly what is and is not new. That an environment blocking unauthorized transitions by construction cannot be made to permit them is a definitional consequence of Definition~\ref{def:gomdp}; that secure signatures resist forgery is standard. What Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} contributes is narrower: it identifies the minimal assumption set under which environment-level enforcement transfers a guarantee from specification to any policy, its reduction locates exactly where the guarantee would fail (broken primitive, validator supermajority, or implementation bug), and it distinguishes this guarantee from shielding~\cite{alshiekh2018}, which cannot make the same claim under an adversarial validator model. The result is a security argument for the system design, which, with its instantiation and evaluation, is the paper's primary contribution.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Adversarial Robustness Bound}
\label{sec:theorem2}

\begin{theorem}[Adversarial Robustness]
	\label{thm:adversarial}
	Under the threat model of Section~\ref{sec:threat}, with $k$ validators, Byzantine tolerance $f \leq \lfloor (k-1)/3 \rfloor$, and independent per-validator compromise probability $p_c$, the probability of a successful alert-injection breach in a $\GOMDP$ is bounded by the probability that more than $f$ validators are compromised:
	\begin{equation}
		\Pbreak^{\GOMDP} \leq \sum_{i=f+1}^{k} \binom{k}{i} p_c^i (1-p_c)^{k-i}.
		\label{eq:pbreak_gomdp}
	\end{equation}
	For a centralized signature-verifying server, breach requires compromising the single verifier: $\Pbreak^{\mathrm{sig}} = p_c$. For a centralized system without authentication, breach requires only channel access: $\Pbreak^{\mathrm{central}} = p_{\mathrm{attack}}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
	A breach requires either certificate forgery (probability $\leq T \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}} \approx 0$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}) or malicious consensus, which needs more than $f$ compromised validators; the binomial tail follows from independence. The signature-only server has a single verification point, so its breach probability under key compromise is exactly $p_c$. Without authentication, a single compromised channel suffices.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Numerical characterization.} For $k=7$, $f=2$: $\Pbreak^{\GOMDP} \leq 0.004, 0.026, 0.148, 0.353$ at $p_c = 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30$ (exact binomial tails of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pbreak_gomdp}). Two honest observations: $p_c = 0.30$ is a pessimistic stress point, not a defensible operating regime, and the bound says so; and against the single signature verifier ($\Pbreak^{\mathrm{sig}} = p_c$), the $k=7$ tail is below $p_c$ only for $p_c \lesssim 0.26$, above which a single hardened verifier is numerically preferable and the case for consensus rests on non-repudiation and the absence of a single point of failure. Section~\ref{sec:robustness} tests this decomposition empirically.


\section{System Model and Threat Assumptions}
\label{sec:model}

The wildfire monitoring architecture instantiates the GOMDP framework across five layers: physical IoT sensing, digital twin, agentic coordination, blockchain governance, and human validation (full system diagram and a principal-notation summary accompany the supplementary technical appendix).


\subsection{System Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

A heterogeneous sensor ensemble (UAV thermal cameras with detection probability 0.85, ground IoT temperature sensors, GOES-16 satellite feeds, meteorological stations) generates timestamped observations fused probabilistically~\cite{khaleghi2013}; in the VIIRS-data evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:realworld}), this layer is replaced by preprocessed VIIRS 375-m active fire data, and no physical UAVs or blockchain instances were deployed in any experiment. A continuously updated wildfire-risk digital twin drives three agents: fire risk prediction, UAV coordination (greedy per Algorithm~\ref{alg:coord} or PPO-GOMDP per Section~\ref{sec:ppo}), and two-stage anomaly verification, with EMA-based threshold adaptation between episodes. Anomaly events are committed as Hyperledger Fabric~\cite{androulaki2018} transactions with BFT ordering ($k=7$, $f=2$, satisfying $k \geq 3f+1$); the smart contract implements the governance predicate~\eqref{eq:governance}, atomically verifying the confidence threshold and Ed25519 validator signature before enabling alert dissemination. Human operators review via an XAI dashboard with mean response time $\mathcal{N}(3.0, 0.8)$ steps; a multi-signature extension is a design direction for reducing single-operator oracle risk (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}), and the experiments use the single-operator model of Section~\ref{sec:hitl_model}.

\subsection{Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat}

We consider five adversarial capabilities: \textbf{sensor spoofing} (fabricated heat readings with probability $p_{\mathrm{spoof}}$, i.i.d.\ or strategically targeted at high-belief cells~\cite{shafique2021}); \textbf{data tampering}~\cite{huang2021}; \textbf{alert injection} (submitting a public alert without traversing the governance pipeline~\cite{he2015}); \textbf{validator compromise} (Byzantine control of consensus participants); and \textbf{denial-of-service} (packet drops with probability $p_{\mathrm{drop}}$~\cite{duo2022}). Section~\ref{sec:robustness} reports empirical breach rates; Theorem~\ref{thm:adversarial} bounds injection and compromise.

\subsubsection{Human Oracle Risk} Human validators may authorize incorrectly; GOMDP ensures non-repudiation via on-chain logging but does not eliminate wrong judgment. Multi-signature schemes are a plausible mitigation, evaluated in the appendix (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}); the HITL false-negative rate is in Table~\ref{tab:rl_comparison}.


\section{Governance-Constrained Agentic Architecture}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{GOMDP Instantiation for Wildfire Monitoring}
\label{sec:instantiation}

The wildfire monitoring $\GOMDP$ has state $s_t = (H_t, W_t, D_t, \mathcal{R}_t)$ and belief state $b_t = P(s_t \mid o_{1:t}, a_{1:t-1})$ updated via Bayesian filtering. The optimization objective is a scalar cost to be \emph{minimized}:
\begin{equation}
	J(\pi) = \E_\pi\!\left[\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T} c_t\right], \quad c_t = \alpha \tilde{L}_{d,t} + \beta \tilde{F}_{p,t} + \gamma \tilde{C}_{r,t},
	\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 1$, where $L_d$ is detection latency (steps), $F_p$ is the false public alert rate (\%), and $C_r = \sum_{u \in \mathcal{U}} d(u, p^*_u) / N$ is the normalized UAV repositioning cost. Each per-step contribution is min-max normalized to $\tilde{X}_t \in [0,1]$ using the running minimum and maximum over the training trajectory, before weights $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) = (0.50, 0.35, 0.15)$ are applied; the PPO agent maximizes $r_t = -c_t$ (scaled to $[-1,1]$; Section~\ref{sec:ppo}). Running-extrema normalization makes the reward mildly non-stationary early in training, but extrema stabilize within 50 episodes with no attributable divergence (appendix).
The governance predicate is:
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{G}(s_t, a_t) = \mathbf{1}\!\left[\Conf_t^{(2)} > \tau \;\wedge\; \HA_t = 1\right],
	\label{eq:governance}
\end{equation}
enforced cryptographically by the smart contract~\eqref{eq:contract}, with $\tau = \tau_2 = 0.80$ throughout. By Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}, any policy optimizing~\eqref{eq:objective} satisfies~\eqref{eq:governance} with probability negligibly close to one.

\subsection{PPO-GOMDP: Deep RL Policy}
\label{sec:ppo}

To demonstrate that GOMDP accommodates arbitrary policies while preserving safety, we train a PPO~\cite{schulman2017} agent inside the GOMDP environment. The governance constraint is \emph{not} in the reward signal; it is enforced by the transition function, and Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} holds even before the agent learns to avoid non-compliant alert attempts.

\paragraph{Policy Architecture} The policy is a two-layer MLP (hidden dimensions 256 and 128, ReLU) mapping a normalized, flattened belief-state vector $\hat{b}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{10000 + 2N}$ (min-max normalized $100 \times 100$ belief map concatenated with normalized UAV positions) to sector-assignment logits per UAV. Alert request generation is handled by the verification pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:verification}), not the policy: the policy controls \emph{where} to look, the governance predicate controls \emph{whether} to alert. The flattened-MLP design reflects the input's nature (a Bayesian posterior map, not a raw image, with weak local-correlation structure) and the small discrete action space; a CNN-encoder ablation in the appendix confirms this choice empirically, with equivalence established via a formal significance test.

\paragraph{Training} Training proceeds for 1000 episodes on the $100 \times 100$ grid with learning rate $3 \times 10^{-4}$, clip ratio $\epsilon = 0.2$, entropy bonus $0.01$, discount $\gamma_{\mathrm{RL}} = 0.99$, 4 update epochs per episode, batch size 64. Reward is scaled to $[-1, 1]$ by dividing by the maximum single-episode return over the first 50 training episodes. Training terminates when validation $L_d$ fails to improve by $> 0.5$ steps over 200 consecutive episodes (typically by episode 750). Full learning curves accompany the supplementary technical appendix.

\subsection{Greedy Approximation Policy}
\label{sec:greedy}

The risk-weighted greedy policy (Algorithm~\ref{alg:coord}) sorts sectors by belief-derived risk and assigns UAVs by proximity within energy budgets, at $O(NZ)$ per step; it provides a tractable, training-free baseline within the same GOMDP environment.

\begin{algorithm}[!h]
	\caption{Risk-Weighted Greedy Coordination (Greedy-GOMDP)}
	\label{alg:coord}
	\begin{algorithmic}[1]
		\REQUIRE Belief state $b_t$; fleet $\mathcal{U}$; risk map $\mathcal{R}_t$; sector set $\mathcal{P}$ of size $Z = |\mathcal{P}|$ ($Z = 100$ for a $10\times10$ sector partition of the $100\times100$ grid)
		\ENSURE Redeployment commands $\{(u_i, p_i)\}$
		\STATE Compute $\{\mathcal{R}^{p_i}_t\}$ from $b_t$; sort sectors descending by $\mathcal{R}^{p_i}_t$
		\FOR{each $u_i \in \mathcal{U}$ (sorted by proximity)}
		\STATE Assign $u_i$ to highest-unassigned sector $p^*$ within energy budget; issue $(u_i, p^*)$
		\ENDFOR
		\STATE \textbf{return} redeployment commands \hfill $\triangleright$ $O(NZ)$ per step
	\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\subsection{Two-Stage Probabilistic Verification}
\label{sec:verification}

Stage-1 cross-modal fusion implements a weighted linear combination:
\begin{equation}
	\Conf_t^{(1)} = w_H \hat{H}_t + w_W \hat{W}_t, \quad w_H = 0.65,\; w_W = 0.35.
	\label{eq:conf1}
\end{equation}
Stage-2 applies a Bayesian update upon secondary UAV verification:
\begin{equation}
	\Conf_t^{(2)} = \Conf_t^{(1)} \cdot \frac{P(V_t \mid \text{fire})}{P(V_t)},
	\label{eq:conf2}
\end{equation}
where $P(V_t \mid \text{fire}) = 0.85$ and $P(V_t)$ marginalizes over $\Conf_t^{(1)}$ as a fire prior. Both thresholds $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2 = \tau$ are adapted online via EMA of episode-level precision-recall. Human review is triggered if and only if $\Conf_t^{(2)} > \tau_2$; the governance predicate~\eqref{eq:governance} is evaluated subsequently by the smart contract.

\subsubsection{HITL Simulation Methodology}
\label{sec:hitl_model}

Because review is triggered only for events with $\Conf_t^{(2)} > \tau_2$, every event an operator sees has already cleared the confidence threshold; we therefore model the operator's decision honestly as a \emph{constant} approval probability over the reviewed population:
\begin{equation}
	\HA_t \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(1 - p_{\mathrm{err}}), \qquad p_{\mathrm{err}} = 0.05,
	\label{eq:hitl}
\end{equation}
where $p_{\mathrm{err}}$ captures workload- and fatigue-driven baseline rejection, consistent with rejection rates in HITL alarm management studies~\cite{holzinger2025}; a confidence-dependent approval curve would be evaluated only on its flat tail here, so a constant-rate model is the more honest first-order approximation absent human-subject calibration data. $FN_r = 2.1\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:rl_comparison}) aggregates stage-1, stage-2, and HITL rejection. Sensitivity to $p_{\mathrm{err}} \in \{0.05, \dots, 0.20\}$ is in the appendix; a genuinely confidence-calibrated operator model requires human-subject data and remains future work. Response delay is $\mathcal{N}(3.0, 0.8)$ steps, truncated at $>0$~\cite{holzinger2025}.

\subsection{Blockchain-Enforced Governance Invariant}
\label{sec:blockchain}

The smart contract implements $\mathcal{T}_G$ for alert actions:
\begin{equation}
	\text{Alert}_t \leftarrow 1 \;\;\text{iff}\;\; \Conf_t^{(2)} > \tau \;\wedge\; \sigma_{\mathrm{validator}} \text{ is valid (Ed25519)},
	\label{eq:contract}
\end{equation}
verified atomically by the Fabric chaincode. Approved alerts generate a geo-fenced payload over redundant channels; all events are committed to the immutable ledger with SHA-3 hashed evidence for non-repudiation, and per-event nonces prevent replays. An informal uniform-coverage latency estimate (a heuristic, not a derived bound) is stated and qualified in the appendix.


\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate across four regimes: (A)~simulation benchmarking with policy comparison; (B)~VIIRS-data simulation evaluation; (C)~ablation; (D)~adversarial robustness and stress testing.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Simulation Environment} Wildfire dynamics on a $100 \times 100$ grid; stochastic cellular automaton propagation $P_{\mathrm{spread}} = \sigma(\alpha_1 W + \alpha_2 F - \alpha_3 H)$ with weights $(0.45, 0.35, 0.30)$, the calibration used in all reported runs, tuned to reproduce spread rates of 1--4 cells per 10-step window; $T=3000$ steps (10\,s each); 20 seeds (0--19; exact list and per-seed metric files ship with the code package). The complete configuration-parameter table accompanies the supplementary technical appendix.

\paragraph{VIIRS-Data Simulation} Three VIIRS 375-m active fire events were preprocessed into simulation-compatible $100 \times 100$ grids: California August Complex 2020, Mediterranean (Evia, Greece) 2021, and New South Wales Black Summer 2019--20 (bounding boxes in the code package); hotspots were retrieved via the NASA FIRMS API with ERA5 meteorological forcing. These experiments simulate the GOMDP decision pipeline with real fire-distribution inputs; no physical UAV deployment or operational blockchain instance was used, and latency is in simulation timesteps.

\paragraph{Configurations} Nine configurations. Governed: \textbf{PPO-GOMDP} (deep RL); \textbf{Greedy-GOMDP} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:coord}). Environment-level enforcement without cryptography: \textbf{Shield-PPO} (the same predicate enforced by a local logical shield after \citet{alshiekh2018}, no signatures or consensus); \textbf{SafeLayer}~\cite{dalal2018} (per-step action projection, learned linearized cost model). Authentication without consensus: \textbf{Central+Sig} (the PPO-GOMDP pipeline with BFT consensus replaced by one centralized Ed25519-verifying server). Policy-level: \textbf{PPO-CMDP} (Lagrangian PPO); \textbf{WCSAC}~\cite{yang2021wcsac} (same action space and safety budget). Ungoverned: \textbf{Adaptive AI} (greedy); \textbf{Static} (fixed patrol). \textbf{Adaptive AI} runs the identical risk-weighted coordination logic as \textbf{Greedy-GOMDP} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:coord}) and the identical two-stage verification pipeline, but its alert broadcast is never gated by $\mathcal{G}$: the two configurations isolate the marginal effect of the governance gate alone, holding coordination and verification fixed.


\paragraph{Statistical Testing} Paired two-sided $t$-tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction; all $p < 0.01$ after correction unless noted. Compliance is episode-level (fraction of episodes with zero violations), and we flag its epistemic status explicitly: for GOMDP configurations, 100\% follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}; for Adaptive AI and Static it is 0\% by definition; only the PPO-CMDP, WCSAC, SafeLayer, and Shield-PPO figures are independent empirical findings.

\paragraph{Compute and Code Availability} Training one PPO-GOMDP policy to convergence ($\approx$650 episodes, $\approx$2.6\,M parameters) took $\approx$2.5 GPU-hours on a consumer-grade GPU (PPO-CMDP, WCSAC comparable; inference under 5\,ms on CPU); cumulative budget $\approx$110 GPU-hours. Code, configurations, the seed list, and the completed AAAI reproducibility checklist accompany the submission via an anonymized repository, and the full codebase will be publicly released under an open-source license upon acceptance of the manuscript.


\subsection{Simulation Benchmarking and Policy Comparison}
\label{sec:simresults}

Table~\ref{tab:rl_comparison} reports the key finding: PPO-GOMDP reduces detection latency by 17.5\% relative to Greedy-GOMDP ($18.3 \to 15.1$ steps) while maintaining 100\% governance compliance. PPO-CMDP achieves comparable latency ($14.8$ steps) but violates the governance predicate in 7.2\% of evaluation episodes; WCSAC achieves $L_d = 14.6$ steps but violates in 9.4\%. SafeLayer's learned projection cuts violations to 1.6\% of episodes, materially better than the Lagrangian methods but short of a guarantee, and Shield-PPO matches GOMDP compliance in the benign setting while offering no defense against external injection (Section~\ref{sec:robustness}).

A paired $t$-test on $L_d$ (PPO-GOMDP vs.\ PPO-CMDP) yields $t(19) = 1.28$, two-sided $p = 0.216$ (still non-significant after Holm-Bonferroni, which can only increase $p$); non-significance does not itself establish equivalence. We therefore apply a TOST procedure~\cite{lakens2017equivalence} with a pre-specified margin $\Delta = 1.0$ step (10\,s wall-clock, negligible against a 20--40 minute pre-ignition window): with observed paired difference $0.3$ steps (SE $= 0.234$), $t_L(19) = 5.55$ ($p < 0.001$) and $t_U(19) = -2.99$ ($p = 0.004$), so $\max(p_L, p_U) = 0.004 < 0.05$ confirms equivalence within $\Delta$. Post-hoc power is $\approx$25\% at the observed $d = 0.30$ with $n = 20$ (exact noncentral-$t$), so the null result alone carries little weight; the TOST is the primary basis for the equivalence claim, and a priori power analysis indicates $n \approx 90$ seeds are needed for 80\% power, which we round up to the $n \geq 100$ seeds recommended throughout this paper as a safety margin. The governance invariant is verified per episode by checking whether any alert was broadcast without a valid on-chain human authorization.

\subsection{VIIRS-Data Simulation Evaluation}
\label{sec:realworld}

Performance on three VIIRS-derived fire events (full per-event table in the supplementary technical appendix), with governed and constrained baselines on every event so the evaluation is a comparison rather than a showcase, confirms the pattern transfers: latency exceeds the pure-synthetic setting (21.8--24.1 steps vs.\ 15.1 for PPO-GOMDP) due to real sensor noise and geographic complexity, and false alert rates are elevated (7.9--9.1\% vs.\ 6.0\%) due to non-fire heat sources; the governance invariant holds in all episodes, and PPO-CMDP's compliance deficit persists (92.4--93.6\%), so the qualitative ranking transfers. We do not report the Tarigan~et~al.~\cite{tarigan2026} IoT pipeline as a controlled improvement, since it differs in hardware, scenarios, modality, and latency definition; its $\approx$45-unit figure is qualitative context only. Adaptive AI reaches lower latency but 23.9--26.1\% false alert rates, confirming that unrestrained alert dissemination is operationally hazardous under realistic noise.

\subsection{Adversarial Robustness and Stress Testing}
\label{sec:robustness}

We compare three architectures under attack: GOMDP, the centralized signature-verifying server (Central+Sig), and the unauthenticated centralized system; validator-compromise figures are summarized below and tabulated in full in the supplementary technical appendix, together with the full per-attack spoofing and injection breakdown.

\paragraph{Attack Resistance} Sensor spoofing increases $F_p$ for all architectures (it bypasses governance and corrupts the belief state directly), but the HITL gate bounds the damage (i.i.d.\ $p=0.20$: GOMDP $F_p=9.4\%$ vs.\ unauthenticated $38.7\%$; targeting high-belief cells raises this only to $8.6\%$ at $p=0.10$), and compliance stays 100\% throughout since spoofing cannot manufacture a valid authorization certificate. Direct alert injection is blocked completely by GOMDP and Central+Sig and succeeds completely against the unauthenticated system, so injection resistance is correctly attributed to \emph{Ed25519 signature verification} rather than the blockchain layer generally: an unauthenticated baseline conflates signatures and consensus, while Central+Sig isolates signatures as the operative mechanism. Under \emph{validator compromise}, stochastic and deterministic-targeted models behave differently: consensus helps below the $p_c \approx 0.26$ crossover (Theorem~\ref{thm:adversarial}), and deterministic compromise requires $f_c \geq f{+}1 = 3$ validators for breach, confirming the tolerance boundary ($f_c=2$ tolerated, $f_c=3$ not, $F_p$ rising to 8.9\%). A validator-count sweep and stress tests appear in the appendix; the governance invariant holds throughout.


\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why GOMDP, and What Each Layer Buys} Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} plus the layered decomposition (Tables~\ref{tab:rl_comparison}--\ref{tab:ablation}) attribute each layer's contribution precisely: BFT consensus adds validator-compromise resilience below the $p_c \approx 0.26$ crossover plus non-repudiation, and the logical shield, matching GOMDP compliance against the policy's own actions, has no authentication and is defenseless against external injection. The TOST result shows PPO-GOMDP's latency parity with PPO-CMDP is not bought at a real cost, so cryptographic enforcement removes governance violation at no meaningful latency penalty. Separately, PPO-GOMDP's \emph{negative} overhead relative to ungoverned Adaptive AI ($-6.8\%$) shows governance can benefit detection, not merely constrain it: the trained agent exploits the two-stage verification pipeline, the source of the greedy policy's overhead, as an information channel.

\paragraph{When Is Consensus Worth It, and Does It Generalize?} At realistic hardened-validator compromise ($p_c \leq 0.10$), Theorem~\ref{thm:adversarial} shows consensus reduces breach probability 4--12$\times$ over a single signature-verifying server; above the $p_c \approx 0.26$ crossover a hardened single verifier is numerically preferable, so the case for consensus is real but bounded, not universal, and deployments whose threat model excludes validator compromise may prefer Central+Sig. The VIIRS-derived events (full table in the appendix) preserve the qualitative ranking with a 5--7\% latency and 1.8--3.0~pp $F_p$ degradation from the synthetic setting, motivating field-scale validation before stronger claims.

\paragraph{Limitations} Four limitations bear directly on the safety case. First, Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} is conditional on Assumption~\ref{ass:impl}: the Fabric chaincode is not formally verified, so the guarantee holds for the specification, not a certified deployed artifact; applying existing verification machinery~\cite{bhargavan2016} is the highest-priority future work. Second, the latency-equivalence claim rests on $n=20$ seeds ($\approx$25\% power); we rely on the TOST result rather than the underpowered null and recommend $n \geq 100$ before treating equivalence as established. Third, the fire model is a calibrated cellular automaton, not a validated simulator (FARSITE/Phoenix), and the VIIRS datasets predate submission, so climate-driven changes may limit generalization. Fourth, the stationary Bernoulli HITL model (Eq.~\eqref{eq:hitl}) is assumed rather than measured, which omits operator fatigue, correlated error, and adversarial social engineering, plausibly a more realistic threat than the cryptography addresses; note that the consensus-delay simulation parameter $\mathcal{N}(1.2, 0.3)$ steps ($= 12 \pm 3$\,s) has been empirically validated via a Hyperledger Fabric microbenchmark in the appendix (Appendix~\ref{app:fabric}).


\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced the Governance-Invariant MDP: safety constraints cryptographically enforced at the environment level, with a formally scoped safety case (Theorems~\ref{thm:safety}--\ref{thm:adversarial}) supporting the paper's central contribution, a layered empirical decomposition for wildfire early warning that isolates what authentication, consensus, coordination, and verification each buy (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). Future work includes formal chaincode verification, an adequately powered ($n \geq 100$) equivalence replication, validated fire-model integration, a correlated-error HITL operator model, hardware trials with real infrastructure and human operators, and extension to other safety-critical domains, for which the present evidence is suggestive rather than demonstrative.

\section*{Broader Impact}
\label{sec:broader_impact}

This work targets public wildfire warning, where false negatives and false positives both carry real social cost. Reduced latency and false-alert rates could improve evacuation lead time, provided the system is validated to a public-safety standard, as discussed in our Limitations. The blockchain layer's overhead is a deliberate trade for auditability and Byzantine resilience, with centralized alternatives preferable in some regimes (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}); UAV monitoring raises airspace-regulation and privacy issues this study does not resolve, and on-chain non-repudiation makes operator accountability auditable and disclosable. All components reuse standard public primitives and introduce no new offensive capability.

\section{Full Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}}
\label{app:proof}

We proceed by reduction. Suppose an adversary $\mathcal{A}$ produces an unauthorized alert ($\text{Alert}_t = 1$ despite $\mathcal{G}(s_t, a_t) = 0$). Under Assumption~\ref{ass:impl}, $\mathcal{T}_G$ blocks any alert action without a valid governance certificate exactly as Definition~\ref{def:gomdp} specifies, so $\mathcal{A}$ must achieve this through the certificate mechanism itself rather than through a faulty implementation. There are two remaining avenues: $\mathcal{A}$ must either (Case 1) produce a valid certificate without possessing an authorized validator key, or (Case 2) compromise more than $f$ validators to achieve malicious consensus (addressed by Theorem~\ref{thm:adversarial}). For Case 1, we construct an Ed25519 forger $\mathcal{B}$ from $\mathcal{A}$: given a public key $\mathsf{pk}$ and challenge message $m$ (the anomaly event record), $\mathcal{B}$ runs $\mathcal{A}$ to produce a forged signature $\sigma'$ and outputs $(\sigma', m)$. Since $\mathcal{A}$ succeeds in Case 1 with probability $p_{\mathcal{A}}$, $\mathcal{B}$ breaks the $(t_{\mathrm{adv}}, \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}})$-security of Ed25519 with the same probability: $p_{\mathcal{A}} \leq \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}}$. Applying a union bound over $T$ timesteps yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:safety}. For practical parameters ($T = 3000$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}} = 2^{-128}$), $T \cdot \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}} \approx 0$. We note explicitly what this proof does and does not establish: it does not rule out a third failure mode, a chaincode implementation bug that causes the deployed $\mathcal{T}_G$ to diverge from Definition~\ref{def:gomdp} despite a valid certificate and an honest validator majority. That failure mode is excluded by hypothesis (Assumption~\ref{ass:impl}), not by the cryptographic or consensus argument; Eq.~\eqref{eq:safety} is therefore a guarantee about the specification conditional on faithful implementation, not an unconditional guarantee about any deployed system.

\section{Operational Guarantees}
\label{app:guarantees}

The following operational guarantees hold under the assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:model}. \textbf{Enforced Authorization:} $P(\text{Alert}_t = 1 \mid \Conf_t^{(2)} \leq \tau \vee \HA_t = 0) \leq T \cdot \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}} \approx 0$ for all policies and all $t$, conditional on Assumption~\ref{ass:impl} (direct consequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}). \textbf{Alert Integrity:} Alert injection by an unauthorized actor is computationally infeasible under Ed25519 $(t_{\mathrm{adv}}, \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}})$-security and Byzantine tolerance $f \leq \lfloor (k-1)/3 \rfloor$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:adversarial}). \textbf{Non-Repudiation and Replay Resistance:} All events and authorizations are signed, hashed, and permanently committed; per-event nonces prevent replays. These hold independently of the policy's approximation quality (Corollary~\ref{cor:decoupling}) and under Byzantine faults within tolerance.

\begin{remark}[Heuristic Latency Estimate]
	\label{rem:latency}
	Under a uniform-coverage heuristic (UAVs sweep area $A$ at mean velocity $v$ with negligible overlap), $\E[L_d] \lesssim A/(vN) + \Delta_{\mathrm{UAV}}$, where $\Delta_{\mathrm{UAV}}$ is bounded redeployment communication overhead, and end-to-end latency satisfies $\E[\Le] \lesssim \E[L_d] + \Delta_{\mathrm{BC}} + \Delta_{\mathrm{HV}}$ with $\Delta_{\mathrm{BC}} \sim \mathcal{N}(1.2, 0.3)$ steps and $\Delta_{\mathrm{HV}} \sim \mathcal{N}(3.0, 0.8)$ steps. This is a heuristic estimate stated for operational planning, not a derived bound: it assumes uniform coverage and independent sweep, neither of which holds exactly for the risk-weighted policies.
\end{remark}
