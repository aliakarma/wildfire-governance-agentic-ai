\begin{abstract}
Wildfire early warning must be fast enough to save lives yet accountable enough to trust: a false public alert can trigger a needless mass evacuation, while a missed detection can be fatal, so the authority to issue an alert cannot be ceded to an unaccountable autonomous agent. Constrained reinforcement learning (RL), the standard tool for safe autonomy, enforces safety only in expectation and thus permits violations. We introduce the \emph{Governance-Invariant MDP} (GOMDP), which makes mandatory human authorization a hard, auditable, non-repudiable constraint enforced at the environment level as a cryptographic state-transition invariant rather than a policy penalty. Instantiated for wildfire monitoring with a Hyperledger Fabric smart contract and evaluated on three real fire events derived from NASA VIIRS/FIRMS data, GOMDP lets no public alert issue without a valid human authorization: under stated assumptions, any policy, however suboptimal or adversarial, satisfies the governance predicate with probability negligibly close to one (Theorem~1), and a closed-form breach bound (Theorem~2) delimits when Byzantine consensus outperforms a single verifier. A layered decomposition isolates what each enforcement layer buys. Across 20 seeds, PPO-GOMDP cuts detection latency by 17.5\% versus a training-free baseline ($18.3\to15.1$ steps) at 100\% governance compliance and cuts false public alerts from 22.4\% to 6.0\%, with statistically equivalent latency to constrained RL (TOST, $p=0.004$) and robustness under adversarial stress. GOMDP is a deployable, auditable accountability layer for safety-critical civic AI.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Wildfire early warning is shifting from human monitoring toward autonomous, agentic AI that fuses satellite, UAV, and sensor data to decide when to raise a public alarm. The stakes are asymmetric: a false alert can trigger a costly, dangerous mass evacuation, while a missed detection can cost lives, so the decision to broadcast must stay accountable to a human authority and auditable. Constrained MDPs~\cite{altman1999} and trust-region methods such as CPO~\cite{achiam2017} enforce constraints only in expectation via Lagrangian relaxation, so violations remain common (5--15\% even after convergence~\cite{achiam2017}); projection-based updates~\cite{yang2020pcpo}, distributional critics~\cite{yang2021wcsac}, and safety layers~\cite{dalal2018} reduce but cannot eliminate them, since all tie their guarantee to policy or model quality~\cite{gu2022survey}. For public warning, an in-expectation, unaccountable guarantee is insufficient.

We address this gap with the \emph{Governance-Invariant MDP} (GOMDP), in which the safety constraint is embedded in the environment's transition function via cryptographic enforcement rather than in the policy's reward signal. The structural insight is a separation of concerns: \emph{safety is an environment property, not a policy property}. Any policy (random, greedy, or fully trained) satisfies the governance constraint with probability negligibly close to one, because the constraint is a cryptographic state-transition invariant no action can bypass. The guarantee is scoped: it secures \emph{authorization integrity and accountability} (no alert without a valid, non-repudiable human authorization), not the correctness of the human decision, which no cryptography can supply. The formal results identify the precise assumptions under which this guarantee holds; the experiments carry the paper's primary evidentiary weight.

We instantiate the framework for wildfire early warning~\cite{divirgilio2019,ghali2022}: a Hyperledger Fabric~\cite{androulaki2018} smart contract refuses to execute the alert-broadcast transition unless a cryptographically valid human authorization signature is present, turning human oversight from a procedural check into an environment constraint for which a fire agency's duty officer, not the autonomous agent, remains the named authorizer, fitting existing emergency-operations authorization chains. It integrates multi-agent UAV coordination (a greedy policy and a deep PPO policy trained inside the GOMDP) with two-stage Bayesian anomaly verification.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} \textbf{(C1)}~\emph{Safety--optimality decoupling for agentic RL}: we formalize the GOMDP, an MDP whose safety constraint is a cryptographic environment-level invariant, and show (Corollary~\ref{cor:decoupling}) that the safety certificate is independent of the policy's optimality gap, unlike CMDP/CPO/WCSAC, whose violation probability scales with that gap. \textbf{(C2)}~\emph{A layered empirical methodology} that separates the marginal contribution of cryptographic authentication from that of Byzantine consensus (the Central+Sig ablation), absent from prior shielding and safety-layer evaluations, and that quantifies what governance costs: because the gate acts after detection, enforcement leaves detection latency unchanged and adds $\approx$4.2 steps to the detection-to-broadcast path, while cutting the false public alert rate from 22.4\% to 6.0\% at full compliance. \textbf{(C3)}~\emph{A formally scoped safety case}: Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} identifies the minimal assumption set under which any policy satisfies the governance predicate with probability negligibly close to one, and Theorem~\ref{thm:adversarial} gives a closed-form breach bound with an explicit envelope for when Byzantine consensus does and does not outperform a single signature-verifying server. \textbf{(C4)}~\emph{A broad evaluation} against constrained RL (PPO-CMDP, WCSAC), cryptography-free environment enforcement (logical shielding, SafeLayer), and a signature-only server, over three VIIRS-derived events and strategic as well as i.i.d.\ adversaries.


\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Constrained RL and Safe AI} Altman's CMDP framework~\cite{altman1999} enforces constraints via Lagrangian relaxation, providing in-expectation guarantees; CPO~\cite{achiam2017} and RCPO~\cite{tessler2019} reduce but cannot eliminate violations, and PCPO~\cite{yang2020pcpo} projects updates onto the constraint set without removing them. WCSAC~\cite{yang2021wcsac} adds a distributional (CVaR-based) safety critic (90.6\% compliance in our evaluation) but remains policy-level. SafeLayer~\cite{dalal2018} projects actions per step via a learned linearized cost model, stronger than Lagrangian methods but inheriting model error (Section~\ref{sec:simresults}). Safe shielding~\cite{alshiekh2018} filters unsafe actions at the environment boundary, architecturally closest to GOMDP; our shield baseline shows this enforcement is complete against the policy's own actions yet defenseless against external injection. Surveys~\cite{gu2022survey} confirm per-trajectory guarantees under adversarial conditions remain open for policy-level methods; GOMDP extends shielding with cryptographic enforcement and Byzantine-tolerant consensus, a threat model absent from prior shielding work.

\paragraph{Runtime Assurance, CBFs, and Monitoring} The Simplex architecture~\cite{sha2001} pioneered environment-boundary enforcement in aerospace: a verified safety controller overrides an unverified performance controller when a safety envelope is threatened. GOMDP inherits this philosophy but replaces the trusted local monitor with a distributed cryptographic mechanism, changing the threat model from component failure to adversarial compromise. Control Barrier Functions~\cite{ames2019} enforce forward-invariant safe sets for continuous dynamics; the GOMDP predicate is discrete and requires non-repudiation beyond CBF synthesis. Runtime monitoring~\cite{leucker2009} observes and reacts to events, whereas GOMDP embeds enforcement in the transition function itself; shield synthesis from LTL specifications~\cite{alshiekh2018} is orthogonal to how the resulting predicate is enforced at runtime.

\paragraph{AI-Based Wildfire Detection} Contemporary systems apply deep learning and multimodal fusion~\cite{ghali2022}. IoT threshold pipelines~\cite{tarigan2026} report mean detection latency near 45 time units without adaptive patrol, consistent with our Static baseline ($41.5$~steps; context only). Partheepan~et~al.~\cite{partheepan2023} identify governance and accountability as open challenges in autonomous UAV monitoring, the gap GOMDP addresses; constrained safe RL for autonomous driving~\cite{gao2024} tackles a related domain with soft Lagrangian constraints.

\paragraph{Blockchain Accountability and Verified Contracts} Permissioned blockchains such as Hyperledger Fabric enable authenticated, tamper-evident execution with Byzantine fault-tolerant (BFT) ordering, while prior blockchain-based disaster monitoring primarily uses distributed ledgers for post-hoc auditing rather than real-time governance enforcement. Assumption~\ref{ass:impl} connects our guarantee to the smart-contract verification literature~\cite{bhargavan2016}. Human-in-the-loop (HITL) and AI-oversight architectures reduce automation error but treat oversight as an advisory or interface-level check~\cite{holzinger2025}; GOMDP instead makes oversight a hard, non-repudiable environment constraint no policy can bypass.


\section{The Governance-Invariant MDP Framework}
\label{sec:gomdp}

We introduce the GOMDP as a system design for environment-level safety enforcement, together with a formal security argument that makes explicit the assumptions under which the design's central guarantee holds.

\subsection{Formal Definition}
\label{sec:def}

\begin{definition}[Governance-Invariant MDP]
	\label{def:gomdp}
	A Governance-Invariant MDP ($\GOMDP$) is a tuple $(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{T}_G, r, \Omega, O, \mathcal{G})$, where $\mathcal{S}$, $\mathcal{A}$, $r$, $\Omega$, $O$ are standard partially observable MDP (POMDP) components with per-step reward $r: \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A} \to \Rset$, $\mathcal{G}: \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A} \to \{0,1\}$ is a \emph{governance predicate}, and $\mathcal{T}_G$ is the governance-constrained transition function: $\mathcal{T}_G(s' \mid s, a) = \mathcal{T}(s' \mid s, a)$ if $\mathcal{G}(s, a) = 1$ or $a \notin \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{alert}}$, and $\mathcal{T}_G(\text{block} \mid s, a) = 1$ (the alert action is blocked) if $a \in \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{alert}}$ and $\mathcal{G}(s, a) = 0$. This makes $\mathcal{T}_G$ total over $\mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A}$: the governance predicate gates only alert actions, and every non-alert action proceeds under the unconstrained kernel $\mathcal{T}$ regardless of $\mathcal{G}$. The enforcement of $\mathcal{G}$ is \emph{cryptographic}: the environment refuses to execute alert state transitions lacking a cryptographically valid governance certificate, independently of the policy.
\end{definition}

The distinction from a CMDP is structural: a CMDP constraint modifies the learning signal via a Lagrange multiplier, so a sufficiently suboptimal or adversarial policy can still act unsafely, whereas a $\GOMDP$ environment cannot produce a state with $\text{Alert} = 1$ unless $\mathcal{G}(s,a) = 1$. This also separates GOMDP from logical shielding~\cite{alshiekh2018}, which lacks cryptographic non-repudiation and BFT consensus, and from SafeLayer~\cite{dalal2018}, whose learned projection carries model error. The structural separation, not a new cryptographic primitive, is the central claim; the formal result below pins down the assumptions it rests on.

\begin{assumption}[Faithful Implementation]
	\label{ass:impl}
	The deployed smart-contract chaincode is a correct, bug-free realization of $\mathcal{T}_G$ as specified in Definition~\ref{def:gomdp}: for every $(s, a)$ with $a \in \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{alert}}$, the chaincode evaluates $\mathcal{G}(s,a)$ and blocks the transition whenever $\mathcal{G}(s,a) = 0$. Formal program verification of the chaincode, for which established techniques exist~\cite{bhargavan2016}, is outside the scope of this paper; we treat this assumption explicitly and return to its consequences in the Limitations.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Policy-Agnostic Safety}
\label{sec:theorem1}

\begin{theorem}[Policy-Agnostic Safety]
	\label{thm:safety}
	Let $\Sigma = (\mathcal{G}\text{-MDP}, \Pi_{\mathrm{Ed25519}}, \mathcal{V})$ be a GOMDP instantiation satisfying Assumption~\ref{ass:impl}, where $\Pi_{\mathrm{Ed25519}}$ is a $(t_{\mathrm{adv}}, \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}})$-secure digital signature scheme~\cite{bernstein2012ed25519} and $\mathcal{V}$ is a set of $k$ validators of which at most $f$ are Byzantine, with $k \geq 3f + 1$ (equivalently, $f \leq \lfloor (k-1)/3 \rfloor$). Let $\pi$ be any stochastic policy (including arbitrarily suboptimal or adversarially chosen policies) operating within $\Sigma$ with governance predicate $\mathcal{G}(s, a) = [\Conf_t^{(2)} > \tau \wedge \HA_t = 1]$. Then, for any adversary running in time $t \leq t_{\mathrm{adv}}$ and for all horizons $T$:
	\begin{equation}
		P_\pi\!\left(\exists\, t \leq T:\; \text{Alert}_t = 1 \;\wedge\; \big(\Conf_t^{(2)} \leq \tau \;\vee\; \HA_t = 0\big)\right) \leq T \cdot \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}},
		\label{eq:safety}
	\end{equation}
	where $T \cdot \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}} \approx 0$ under standard hardness assumptions for Ed25519 ($\epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}} \approx 2^{-128}$). Conditional on Assumption~\ref{ass:impl}, the guarantee is independent of $\pi$ and holds with probability negligibly close to one over all trajectories.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
	By reduction: an adversary producing an unauthorized alert must, under Assumption~\ref{ass:impl}, either forge a valid certificate without an authorized validator key (Case 1) or corrupt more than $f$ validators (Case 2, handled by Theorem~\ref{thm:adversarial}). Case 1 yields an Ed25519 forger with the same success probability, bounded by $\epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}}$; a union bound over $T$ timesteps gives~\eqref{eq:safety}. The proof does not rule out a chaincode bug; that failure mode is excluded by hypothesis (Assumption~\ref{ass:impl}), so \eqref{eq:safety} is a guarantee about the specification conditional on faithful implementation, not an unconditional guarantee about any deployed artifact. The full proof appears in the technical appendix.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Safety--Optimality Decoupling]
	\label{cor:decoupling}
	The guarantee of Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} holds for any policy $\pi$, including the worst-case policy that minimizes return; the optimality gap does not affect the safety certificate. This contrasts with CMDP/CPO/WCSAC, where constraint violation probability depends directly on the optimality gap during training~\cite{achiam2017,yang2021wcsac}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Novelty and Scope]
	\label{rem:scope}
	The novelty is scoped precisely. That an environment blocking unauthorized transitions by construction cannot be made to permit them is a definitional consequence of Definition~\ref{def:gomdp}; that secure signatures resist forgery is standard. Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} contributes the connective step: it identifies the minimal assumption set under which environment-level enforcement transfers a guarantee from specification to any policy, its reduction locates exactly where the guarantee would fail (broken primitive, validator supermajority, or implementation bug), and it distinguishes this guarantee from shielding~\cite{alshiekh2018}, which cannot make the same claim under an adversarial validator model. The result is a security argument for the system design, which, with its instantiation and evaluation, is the paper's primary contribution.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Adversarial Robustness Bound}
\label{sec:theorem2}

\begin{theorem}[Adversarial Robustness]
	\label{thm:adversarial}
	Under the threat model of Section~\ref{sec:threat}, with $k$ validators, Byzantine tolerance $f \leq \lfloor (k-1)/3 \rfloor$, and independent per-validator compromise probability $p_c$, the probability of a successful alert-injection breach in a $\GOMDP$ is bounded by the probability that more than $f$ validators are compromised:
	\begin{equation}
		\Pbreak^{\GOMDP} \leq \sum_{i=f+1}^{k} \binom{k}{i} p_c^i (1-p_c)^{k-i}.
		\label{eq:pbreak_gomdp}
	\end{equation}
	For a centralized signature-verifying server, breach requires compromising the single verifier: $\Pbreak^{\mathrm{sig}} = p_c$. For a centralized system without authentication, breach requires only channel access: $\Pbreak^{\mathrm{central}} = p_{\mathrm{attack}}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
	A breach requires either certificate forgery (probability $\leq T \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}} \approx 0$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}) or malicious consensus, which needs more than $f$ compromised validators; the binomial tail follows from independence. The signature-only server has a single verification point, so its breach probability under key compromise is exactly $p_c$. Without authentication, a single compromised channel suffices.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Numerical characterization.} For $k=7$, $f=2$: $\Pbreak^{\GOMDP} \leq 0.004, 0.026, 0.148, 0.353$ at $p_c = 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30$ (exact binomial tails of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pbreak_gomdp}). Two observations: $p_c = 0.30$ is a pessimistic stress point, not a defensible operating regime, and the bound reflects this; and against the single signature verifier ($\Pbreak^{\mathrm{sig}} = p_c$), the $k=7$ tail is below $p_c$ only for $p_c \lesssim 0.26$, above which a single hardened verifier is numerically preferable and the case for consensus rests on non-repudiation and the absence of a single point of failure. Section~\ref{sec:robustness} tests this decomposition empirically.

\subsection{System Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} details the pipeline. A heterogeneous sensor ensemble (UAV thermal cameras with detection probability 0.85, ground IoT sensors, GOES-16 feeds, meteorological stations) generates observations fused probabilistically~\cite{khaleghi2013}; in the VIIRS-data evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:realworld}) this layer is replaced by preprocessed VIIRS 375-m active fire data, and no physical UAVs or blockchain instances were deployed in any experiment. A wildfire-risk digital twin drives three agents (fire-risk prediction, UAV coordination via Algorithm~\ref{alg:coord} or PPO-GOMDP, Section~\ref{sec:ppo}, and two-stage anomaly verification), with EMA-based threshold adaptation between episodes. Anomaly events are committed as Hyperledger Fabric~\cite{androulaki2018} transactions under BFT ordering ($k=7$, $f=2$, $k \geq 3f+1$); the smart contract enforces the governance predicate~\eqref{eq:governance}, atomically checking the confidence threshold and Ed25519 validator signature before alert dissemination. Operators review via an explainable-AI (XAI) dashboard (response time $\mathcal{N}(3.0, 0.8)$ steps); a multi-signature extension targets single-operator oracle risk (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}), and the experiments use the single-operator model of Section~\ref{sec:hitl_model}.

\subsection{Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat}

We consider five adversarial capabilities (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}): \textbf{sensor spoofing} (fabricated heat readings with probability $p_{\mathrm{spoof}}$, i.i.d.\ or targeted at high-belief cells~\cite{shafique2021}); \textbf{data tampering}~\cite{huang2021}; \textbf{alert injection} (a public alert bypassing the governance pipeline~\cite{he2015}); \textbf{validator compromise} (Byzantine control of consensus); and \textbf{denial-of-service} (packet drops $p_{\mathrm{drop}}$~\cite{duo2022}). Section~\ref{sec:robustness} reports breach rates; Theorem~\ref{thm:adversarial} bounds injection and compromise.

\subsubsection{Human Oracle Risk} GOMDP secures \emph{authorization integrity} (no alert without a valid, attributable human signature), not \emph{decision correctness}: a validator may authorize incorrectly, and on-chain logging makes such decisions non-repudiable and auditable but does not prevent them. We report robustness to the operator error rate $p_{\mathrm{err}}$ up to $0.20$ (appendix), and an $m$-of-$n$ multi-signature quorum (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) reduces single-operator risk; correlated fatigue and adversarial social engineering remain outside the model. The HITL false-negative rate is in Table~\ref{tab:rl_comparison}.


\section{Governance-Constrained Agentic Architecture}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{GOMDP Instantiation for Wildfire Monitoring}
\label{sec:instantiation}

The wildfire monitoring $\GOMDP$ has state $s_t = (H_t, W_t, D_t, \mathcal{R}_t)$ and belief state $b_t = P(s_t \mid o_{1:t}, a_{1:t-1})$ updated via Bayesian filtering. The optimization objective is a scalar cost to be \emph{minimized}:
\begin{equation}
	J(\pi) = \E_\pi\!\left[\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T} c_t\right], \quad c_t = \alpha \tilde{L}_{d,t} + \beta \tilde{F}_{p,t} + \gamma \tilde{C}_{r,t},
	\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 1$, where $L_d$ is detection latency (steps), $F_p$ is the false public alert rate (\%), and $C_r = \sum_{u \in \mathcal{U}} d(u, p^*_u) / N$ is the normalized UAV repositioning cost. Each per-step contribution is min-max normalized to $\tilde{X}_t \in [0,1]$ using the running minimum and maximum over the training trajectory, before weights $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) = (0.50, 0.35, 0.15)$ are applied; the PPO agent maximizes $r_t = -c_t$ (scaled to $[-1,1]$; Section~\ref{sec:ppo}). Running-extrema normalization makes the reward mildly non-stationary early in training, but extrema stabilize within 50 episodes with no attributable divergence (appendix).
The governance predicate is:
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{G}(s_t, a_t) = \mathbf{1}\!\left[\Conf_t^{(2)} > \tau \;\wedge\; \HA_t = 1\right],
	\label{eq:governance}
\end{equation}
enforced cryptographically by the smart contract~\eqref{eq:contract}, with $\tau = \tau_2 = 0.80$ throughout. By Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}, any policy optimizing~\eqref{eq:objective} satisfies~\eqref{eq:governance} with probability negligibly close to one.

\subsection{PPO-GOMDP: Deep RL Policy}
\label{sec:ppo}

To demonstrate that GOMDP accommodates arbitrary policies while preserving safety, we train a PPO~\cite{schulman2017} agent inside the GOMDP environment. The governance constraint is \emph{not} in the reward signal; it is enforced by the transition function, and Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} holds even before the agent learns to avoid non-compliant alert attempts.

\paragraph{Policy Architecture} The policy is a two-layer MLP (hidden dimensions 256 and 128, ReLU) mapping a normalized, flattened belief-state vector $\hat{b}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{10000 + 2N}$ (min-max normalized $100 \times 100$ belief map concatenated with normalized UAV positions) to sector-assignment logits per UAV. Alert request generation is handled by the verification pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:verification}), not the policy: the policy controls \emph{where} to look, the governance predicate controls \emph{whether} to alert. The flattened-MLP design reflects the input's nature (a Bayesian posterior map, not a raw image, with weak local-correlation structure) and the small discrete action space; we did not evaluate a convolutional encoder, and whether one would improve sample efficiency on this input remains open.

\paragraph{Training} Training proceeds for 1000 episodes on the $100 \times 100$ grid with learning rate $3 \times 10^{-4}$, clip ratio $\epsilon = 0.2$, entropy bonus $0.01$, discount $\gamma_{\mathrm{RL}} = 0.99$, 4 update epochs per episode, batch size 64. Reward is scaled to $[-1, 1]$ by dividing by the maximum single-episode return over the first 50 training episodes. Training terminates when validation $L_d$ fails to improve by $> 0.5$ steps over 100 consecutive episodes (typically by episode 750, with the validation curve plateauing at $L_d \approx 15.1$ by episode 650; the per-episode validation curve accompanies the code package).

\subsection{Greedy Approximation Policy}
\label{sec:greedy}

The risk-weighted greedy policy (Algorithm~\ref{alg:coord}) sorts sectors by belief-derived risk and assigns UAVs by proximity within energy budgets, at $O(NZ)$ per step; it provides a tractable, training-free baseline within the same GOMDP environment.

\begin{algorithm}[!h]
	\caption{Risk-Weighted Greedy Coordination (Greedy-GOMDP)}
	\label{alg:coord}
	\begin{algorithmic}[1]
		\REQUIRE Belief state $b_t$; fleet $\mathcal{U}$; risk map $\mathcal{R}_t$; sector set $\mathcal{P}$ of size $Z = |\mathcal{P}|$ ($Z = 25$ for a $5\times5$ sector partition of the $100\times100$ grid)
		\ENSURE Redeployment commands $\{(u_i, p_i)\}$
		\STATE Compute $\{\mathcal{R}^{p_i}_t\}$ from $b_t$; sort sectors descending by $\mathcal{R}^{p_i}_t$
		\FOR{each $u_i \in \mathcal{U}$ (sorted by proximity)}
		\STATE Assign $u_i$ to highest-unassigned sector $p^*$ within energy budget; issue $(u_i, p^*)$
		\ENDFOR
		\STATE \textbf{return} redeployment commands \hfill $\triangleright$ $O(NZ)$ per step
	\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\subsection{Two-Stage Probabilistic Verification}
\label{sec:verification}

Stage-1 cross-modal fusion implements a weighted linear combination:
\begin{equation}
	\Conf_t^{(1)} = w_H \hat{H}_t + w_W \hat{W}_t, \quad w_H = 0.65,\; w_W = 0.35.
	\label{eq:conf1}
\end{equation}
Stage-2 applies a Bayesian update upon secondary UAV verification:
\begin{equation}
	\Conf_t^{(2)} = \Conf_t^{(1)} \cdot \frac{P(V_t \mid \text{fire})}{P(V_t)},
	\label{eq:conf2}
\end{equation}
where $P(V_t \mid \text{fire}) = 0.85$ and $P(V_t)$ marginalizes over $\Conf_t^{(1)}$ as a fire prior. Both thresholds $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2 = \tau$ are adapted online via EMA of episode-level precision-recall. Human review is triggered if and only if $\Conf_t^{(2)} > \tau_2$; the governance predicate~\eqref{eq:governance} is evaluated subsequently by the smart contract.

\subsubsection{HITL Simulation Methodology}
\label{sec:hitl_model}

Because review is triggered only for events with $\Conf_t^{(2)} > \tau_2$, every event an operator sees has already cleared the confidence threshold; we therefore model the operator's decision as a \emph{constant} approval probability over the reviewed population:
\begin{equation}
	\HA_t \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(1 - p_{\mathrm{err}}), \qquad p_{\mathrm{err}} = 0.05,
	\label{eq:hitl}
\end{equation}
where $p_{\mathrm{err}}$ captures workload- and fatigue-driven baseline rejection, consistent with rejection rates in HITL alarm management studies~\cite{holzinger2025}; a confidence-dependent approval curve would be evaluated only on its flat tail here, so a constant-rate model is the appropriate first-order approximation absent human-subject calibration data. $FN_r = 2.1\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:rl_comparison}) aggregates stage-1, stage-2, and HITL rejection. Sensitivity to $p_{\mathrm{err}} \in \{0.05, \dots, 0.20\}$ is in the appendix; a genuinely confidence-calibrated operator model requires human-subject data and remains future work. Response delay is $\mathcal{N}(3.0, 0.8)$ steps, truncated at $>0$~\cite{holzinger2025}.

\subsection{Blockchain-Enforced Governance Invariant}
\label{sec:blockchain}

The smart contract implements $\mathcal{T}_G$ for alert actions:
\begin{equation}
	\text{Alert}_t \leftarrow 1 \;\;\text{iff}\;\; \Conf_t^{(2)} > \tau \;\wedge\; \sigma_{\mathrm{validator}} \text{ is valid (Ed25519)},
	\label{eq:contract}
\end{equation}
verified atomically by the Fabric chaincode. Approved alerts generate a geo-fenced payload over redundant channels; all events are committed to the immutable ledger with SHA-3 hashed evidence for non-repudiation, and per-event nonces prevent replays.


\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate across four regimes: (A)~simulation benchmarking with policy comparison; (B)~VIIRS-data simulation evaluation; (C)~ablation; (D)~adversarial robustness and stress testing.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Simulation Environment} Wildfire dynamics on a $100 \times 100$ grid; stochastic cellular automaton propagation $P_{\mathrm{spread}} = \sigma(\alpha_1 W + \alpha_2 F - \alpha_3 H)$ with weights $(0.45, 0.35, 0.30)$, the calibration used in all reported runs, tuned to reproduce spread rates of 1--4 cells per 10-step window; $T=3000$ steps (10\,s each); 20 seeds (0--19; per-seed metric files in the code package). The full parameter table is in the appendix.

\paragraph{VIIRS-Data Simulation} Three Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS) 375-m active fire events were preprocessed into $100 \times 100$ grids: California August Complex 2020, Mediterranean (Evia) 2021, and NSW Black Summer 2019--20 (bounding boxes in the code package); hotspots came from the NASA FIRMS API with ERA5 forcing. These experiments run the GOMDP pipeline on real fire-distribution inputs; no physical UAV or blockchain instance was used, and latency is in simulation timesteps.

\paragraph{Configurations} Nine configurations. Governed: \textbf{PPO-GOMDP} (deep RL); \textbf{Greedy-GOMDP} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:coord}). Environment-level enforcement without cryptography: \textbf{Shield-PPO} (the same predicate enforced by a local logical shield after \citet{alshiekh2018}, no signatures or consensus); \textbf{SafeLayer}~\cite{dalal2018} (per-step action projection, learned linearized cost model). Authentication without consensus: \textbf{Central+Sig} (the PPO-GOMDP pipeline with BFT consensus replaced by one centralized Ed25519-verifying server). Policy-level surrogates (see Statistical Testing): \textbf{PPO-CMDP} (Lagrangian-style soft constraint); \textbf{WCSAC}~\cite{yang2021wcsac} (same action space and safety budget). Ungoverned: \textbf{Adaptive AI} (greedy); \textbf{Static} (fixed patrol). \textbf{Adaptive AI} uses the identical coordination (Algorithm~\ref{alg:coord}) and two-stage verification as \textbf{Greedy-GOMDP} but is never gated by $\mathcal{G}$, isolating the marginal effect of the governance gate alone.


\paragraph{Statistical Testing} Paired two-sided $t$-tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction; all $p < 0.01$ unless noted. Compliance is episode-level (fraction of episodes with zero violations); it is definitional for the GOMDP rows (Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}) and for Adaptive AI/Static (0\%), so neither is an independent measurement. The policy-level rows (PPO-CMDP, WCSAC, SafeLayer) are \emph{surrogates} reproducing the enforcement semantics of Lagrangian and projection methods -- a soft constraint admitting residual violations -- not reimplementations; their compliance characterizes in-expectation enforcement in our environment, not those specific published methods, and lands in the 5--15\% residual-violation regime CPO reports~\cite{achiam2017}.

\paragraph{Compute and Code Availability} Training one PPO-GOMDP policy to convergence ($\approx$650 episodes, $\approx$2.6\,M parameters) took $\approx$2.5 GPU-hours on a consumer-grade GPU (PPO-CMDP and WCSAC comparable); cumulative budget $\approx$110 GPU-hours; inference is under 5\,ms on CPU. Code, configurations, preprocessing scripts, and the per-seed metric files are provided as a supplementary code and data archive with this submission.


\subsection{Simulation Benchmarking and Policy Comparison}
\label{sec:simresults}

Table~\ref{tab:rl_comparison} reports the key finding: PPO-GOMDP reduces detection latency by 17.5\% relative to Greedy-GOMDP ($18.3 \to 15.1$ steps) while maintaining 100\% governance compliance. PPO-CMDP achieves comparable latency ($14.8$ steps) but violates the governance predicate in 7.2\% of evaluation episodes; WCSAC achieves $L_d = 14.6$ steps but violates in 9.4\%. SafeLayer's learned projection cuts violations to 1.6\% of episodes, materially better than the Lagrangian methods but short of a guarantee, and Shield-PPO matches GOMDP compliance in the benign setting while offering no defense against external injection (Section~\ref{sec:robustness}).

A paired $t$-test on $L_d$ (PPO-GOMDP vs.\ PPO-CMDP) yields $t(19)=1.28$, two-sided $p=0.216$ (still non-significant after Holm-Bonferroni, which can only increase $p$); non-significance does not itself establish equivalence. We therefore apply a two one-sided tests (TOST) procedure~\cite{lakens2017equivalence} with a pre-specified margin $\Delta=1.0$ step (10\,s wall-clock, negligible against a 20--40 minute pre-ignition window): with observed paired difference $0.3$ steps (SE $=0.234$), $t_L(19)=5.55$ ($p<0.001$) and $t_U(19)=-2.99$ ($p=0.004$), so $\max(p_L,p_U)=0.004<0.05$ confirms equivalence within $\Delta$. Because the governance gate acts only after detection (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}), no latency difference between governed and ungoverned coordination is expected from the mechanism itself; any observed difference reflects the coordination policy, not the enforcement layer. The governance invariant is verified per episode by checking whether any alert was broadcast without a valid on-chain human authorization, and injection resistance is measured by mounting forged and replayed authorization attempts against the live contract rather than assumed (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\subsection{VIIRS-Data Simulation Evaluation}
\label{sec:realworld}

Performance on three VIIRS-derived fire events (full per-event table in the supplementary technical appendix), with governed and constrained baselines on every event so the evaluation is a comparison rather than a showcase, confirms the pattern transfers: latency exceeds the pure-synthetic setting (21.8--24.1 steps vs.\ 15.1 for PPO-GOMDP) due to real sensor noise and geographic complexity, and false alert rates are elevated (7.9--9.1\% vs.\ 6.0\%) due to non-fire heat sources; the governance invariant holds in all episodes, and PPO-CMDP's compliance deficit persists (92.4--93.6\%), so the qualitative ranking transfers. We do not report the Tarigan~et~al.~\cite{tarigan2026} IoT pipeline as a controlled improvement, since it differs in hardware, scenarios, modality, and latency definition; its $\approx$45-unit figure is qualitative context only. Adaptive AI reaches lower latency but 23.9--26.1\% false alert rates, confirming that unrestrained alert dissemination is operationally hazardous under realistic noise.

\subsection{Adversarial Robustness and Stress Testing}
\label{sec:robustness}

We compare three architectures under attack: GOMDP, the centralized signature-verifying server (Central+Sig), and the unauthenticated centralized system; validator-compromise figures are summarized below and tabulated in full in the supplementary technical appendix, together with the full per-attack spoofing and injection breakdown.

\paragraph{Attack Resistance} Sensor spoofing increases $F_p$ for all architectures (it bypasses governance and corrupts the belief state directly), but the HITL gate bounds the damage (i.i.d.\ $p=0.20$: GOMDP $F_p=9.4\%$ vs.\ unauthenticated $38.7\%$; targeting high-belief cells raises this only to $8.6\%$ at $p=0.10$), and compliance stays 100\% throughout since spoofing cannot manufacture a valid authorization certificate. Direct alert injection is blocked completely by GOMDP and Central+Sig and succeeds completely against the unauthenticated system, so injection resistance is correctly attributed to \emph{Ed25519 signature verification} rather than the blockchain layer generally: an unauthenticated baseline conflates signatures and consensus, while Central+Sig isolates signatures as the operative mechanism. Under \emph{validator compromise}, stochastic and deterministic-targeted models behave differently: consensus helps below the $p_c \approx 0.26$ crossover (Theorem~\ref{thm:adversarial}), and deterministic compromise requires $f_c \geq f{+}1 = 3$ validators for breach, confirming the tolerance boundary ($f_c=2$ tolerated, $f_c=3$ not, $F_p$ rising to 8.9\%). A validator-count sweep appears in the appendix; the governance invariant holds throughout.


\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why GOMDP, and What Each Layer Buys} Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} and the layered decomposition (Tables~\ref{tab:rl_comparison}--\ref{tab:ablation}) attribute each layer's role: signature verification alone defeats direct alert injection; BFT consensus adds validator-compromise resilience below the $p_c \approx 0.26$ crossover plus non-repudiation; and the logical shield matches GOMDP compliance against the policy's own actions but, lacking authentication, is defenseless against external injection.

\paragraph{What Governance Costs} The governance gate is evaluated \emph{after} detection and verification, so by construction it cannot change detection latency $L_d$: a policy finds a fire exactly as fast whether or not its alert will subsequently require authorization. What governance costs is the delay between detection and public broadcast, $\Delta_{\mathrm{BC}} + \Delta_{\mathrm{HITL}} \approx 1.2 + 3.0 = 4.2$ steps ($\approx$42\,s at 10\,s per step). Against a 20--40 minute pre-ignition window this is a small price for a non-repudiable authorization record, and it is the honest accounting: the benefit of governance appears in $F_p$ (Table~\ref{tab:rl_comparison}), not in $L_d$. We note this because an apparent latency \emph{improvement} from adding governance would indicate a confound rather than a mechanism.

\paragraph{When Is Consensus Worth It, and Does It Generalize?} At realistic hardened-validator compromise ($p_c \leq 0.10$), Theorem~\ref{thm:adversarial} shows consensus reduces breach probability roughly 4--13$\times$ over a single signature-verifying server; above the $p_c \approx 0.26$ crossover a hardened single verifier is preferable, so the case for consensus is real but bounded. Consensus earns its cost in multi-agency settings (e.g., cross-jurisdictional wildfire response with no single mutually trusted server), where Byzantine agreement over independently operated validators is the minimal trust model. The VIIRS-derived events preserve the qualitative ranking with only modest latency and $F_p$ degradation (appendix), motivating field-scale validation before stronger claims.

\paragraph{Limitations} Five limitations bear on the safety case. First, Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} is conditional on Assumption~\ref{ass:impl}: the chaincode is not formally verified, so the guarantee holds for the specification, not a certified deployment; applying existing verification machinery~\cite{bhargavan2016} is the highest-priority future work. Second, consensus is \emph{simulated}: the Ed25519 signing, key authorization, nonce ledger, and threshold logic are real and exercised by the reported attacks, but BFT ordering is a Python delay model and no Fabric network was deployed, so consensus-layer latency and throughput under real network conditions remain unmeasured. Third, the fire model is a cellular automaton rather than a validated operational simulator (FARSITE/Phoenix), so absolute latencies should be read as within-model comparisons, not field-calibrated values; likewise $F_p$ is a within-model false-discovery ratio inflated by repeat alerts on a spreading fire. Fourth, the policy-level comparators are surrogates reproducing soft-constraint enforcement semantics rather than reimplementations of the published algorithms (Section~\ref{sec:setup}), so they bound the regime rather than benchmark those methods. Fifth, the human-operator model is a stationary Bernoulli approval (Eq.~\eqref{eq:hitl}): the cryptographic guarantee is independent of operator error and the sensitivity sweep covers $p_{\mathrm{err}}$ up to $0.20$ (appendix), but the model omits correlated fatigue and adversarial social engineering, the residual threat the cryptography deliberately does not address, since GOMDP secures \emph{authorization integrity}, not \emph{decision correctness}.


\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced the Governance-Invariant MDP: safety constraints cryptographically enforced at the environment level, with a formally scoped safety case (Theorems~\ref{thm:safety}--\ref{thm:adversarial}) supporting the paper's central contribution: a layered empirical decomposition for wildfire early warning that isolates what authentication, consensus, coordination, and verification each buy (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). The path to deployment is staged (Section~\ref{sec:broader_impact}), from the present simulation on real fire distributions toward supervised field operation; formal chaincode verification, validated fire-simulator integration, and a correlated-error operator model are the technical prerequisites, and the framework extends to other safety-critical domains.

\section*{Broader Impact and Path to Deployment}
\label{sec:broader_impact}

GOMDP addresses a civic need, public wildfire warning, where false alarms and missed detections both carry real social cost. Its contribution is \emph{accountability}: every public-alert decision is provably authorized by a named human operator and permanently, tamper-evidently logged, an audit trail current pipelines lack. Deployment is staged and reaches only stage one here (simulation on real fire distributions); hardware-in-the-loop trials with fire-agency operators and supervised field operation remain future work. UAV monitoring raises airspace and privacy concerns, and all components add no offensive capability.

\section{Full Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}}
\label{app:proof}

We proceed by reduction. Suppose an adversary $\mathcal{A}$ produces an unauthorized alert ($\text{Alert}_t = 1$ despite $\mathcal{G}(s_t, a_t) = 0$). Under Assumption~\ref{ass:impl}, $\mathcal{T}_G$ blocks any alert action without a valid governance certificate exactly as Definition~\ref{def:gomdp} specifies, so $\mathcal{A}$ must achieve this through the certificate mechanism itself rather than through a faulty implementation. There are two remaining avenues: $\mathcal{A}$ must either (Case 1) produce a valid certificate without possessing an authorized validator key, or (Case 2) compromise more than $f$ validators to achieve malicious consensus (addressed by Theorem~\ref{thm:adversarial}). For Case 1, we construct an Ed25519 forger $\mathcal{B}$ from $\mathcal{A}$: given a public key $\mathsf{pk}$ and challenge message $m$ (the anomaly event record), $\mathcal{B}$ runs $\mathcal{A}$ to produce a forged signature $\sigma'$ and outputs $(\sigma', m)$. Since $\mathcal{A}$ succeeds in Case 1 with probability $p_{\mathcal{A}}$, $\mathcal{B}$ breaks the $(t_{\mathrm{adv}}, \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}})$-security of Ed25519 with the same probability: $p_{\mathcal{A}} \leq \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}}$. Applying a union bound over $T$ timesteps yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:safety}. For practical parameters ($T = 3000$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}} = 2^{-128}$), $T \cdot \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}} \approx 0$. We note explicitly what this proof does and does not establish: it does not rule out a third failure mode, a chaincode implementation bug that causes the deployed $\mathcal{T}_G$ to diverge from Definition~\ref{def:gomdp} despite a valid certificate and an honest validator majority. That failure mode is excluded by hypothesis (Assumption~\ref{ass:impl}), not by the cryptographic or consensus argument; Eq.~\eqref{eq:safety} is therefore a guarantee about the specification conditional on faithful implementation, not an unconditional guarantee about any deployed system.

\section{Operational Guarantees}
\label{app:guarantees}

The following operational guarantees hold under the assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:model}. \textbf{Enforced Authorization:} $P(\exists\, t \leq T: \text{Alert}_t = 1 \wedge (\Conf_t^{(2)} \leq \tau \vee \HA_t = 0)) \leq T \cdot \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}} \approx 0$ for all policies, conditional on Assumption~\ref{ass:impl} (direct consequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}). \textbf{Alert Integrity:} Alert injection by an unauthorized actor is computationally infeasible under Ed25519 $(t_{\mathrm{adv}}, \epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}})$-security and Byzantine tolerance $f \leq \lfloor (k-1)/3 \rfloor$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:adversarial}). \textbf{Non-Repudiation and Replay Resistance:} All events and authorizations are signed, hashed, and permanently committed; per-event nonces prevent replays. These hold independently of the policy's approximation quality (Corollary~\ref{cor:decoupling}) and under Byzantine faults within tolerance.

\section{Threshold and HITL Sensitivity}
\label{app:sensitivity}

$F_p$, $L_d$, and $FN_r$ were evaluated over $\tau_1 \in \{0.50, 0.55, 0.60, 0.65, 0.70\}$ and $\tau_2 \in \{0.75, 0.80, 0.85, 0.90\}$. Governance compliance remains 100\% across the entire grid for PPO-GOMDP and Greedy-GOMDP (Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} is threshold-independent). HITL approval volume decreases with $\tau_2$, raising $FN_r$ by up to 1.4~pp at $\tau_2 = 0.90$; the chosen configuration ($\tau_1=0.60$, $\tau_2=0.80$) achieves the best precision-recall balance on the validation seed set, and qualitative rankings are preserved across the grid. Table~\ref{tab:hitl_sensitivity} reports sensitivity to the operator error rate $p_{\mathrm{err}}$: compliance remains 100\% for all values (the cryptographic invariant is independent of operator error), $FN_r$ increases monotonically with $p_{\mathrm{err}}$ as more genuine events are rejected, and $F_p$ decreases slightly as stricter rejection also filters borderline false positives. This tradeoff underscores the importance of calibrating operator rejection in real deployments; a multi-signature ($m$-of-$n$) scheme is a candidate mitigation we have not implemented.

\section{$m$-of-$n$ Multisignature Ablation}
\label{app:multisig}

Section~\ref{sec:ablation} identifies a lightweight $m$-of-$n$ multi-signature quorum as a plausible, lower-overhead alternative to the full Hyperledger Fabric deployment. The design replaces the BFT consensus layer with $m$-of-$n$ threshold signatures collected from $n$ designated verifiers, requiring no blockchain ledger; non-repudiation is retained (each verifier's signature is attributable) but on-chain immutability and Byzantine agreement across a larger validator set are not.
